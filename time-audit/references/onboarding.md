# Onboarding: 9 questions

Ask questions 1 and 2 together in your first message, then give the first read (Step 1), then ask 3 to 9 in a conversation, two or three at a time, not as a form. If you already know an answer from this chat or from memory, say what you found and ask them to confirm instead of asking from scratch. Target: under 7 minutes.

**1. The business.** In one line, what does the business do? What's your role, and who's on the team (including any EA)?

**2. Your income.** Roughly what do you earn from the business per year (salary plus distributions)? This sets your **Buyback Rate**: income ÷ 2,000 ÷ 4. Anything you do that someone could do for less than that rate should leave your plate. If they won't share it, ask what one hour of their time is worth.

**3. Goals.** What are your top 3 outcomes for the next 90 days, ranked?

**4. Only you.** What work would you keep even with unlimited help?

**5. Energy.** What work gives you energy? What drains you, even when it matters?

**6. Your day.** When are your 3–4 peak hours? When does work start and stop, and what fixed personal commitments sit in between?

**7. Your ideal week.** Which days or windows would you give to calls, which to deep work, and is any day fully off?

**8. Untouchables.** Which meetings would you never cut?

**9. Your tools.** Which calendar(s), call recorder and email account do you use? Skip this question if the connectors already answer it. (The first page uses the calendar only; this tells you what to offer in the second pass.)

## After the interview

Write back a one-screen **profile** with: goals, Unique Ability, green/red work, peak window, hard stop, ideal-week sketch, **hourly value and Buyback Rate**, and the targets. Ask "anything wrong here?", fix it, then go on to Step 3 (the calendar is already pulled for the first read). Offer to keep the profile for next time: ask them to paste it into a Claude Project's instructions or save it in a note, so a re-run skips onboarding.
