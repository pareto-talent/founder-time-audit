# The Founder Time System

The best-known time-management systems for entrepreneurs boiled down to ten laws. Each law has a
**check**: the measurement the time audit runs to see whether the founder is actually living it.
Read this before running the audit. The audit is only as good as the laws behind it.

## Sources

| Thinker | Idea used here |
|---|---|
| Peter Drucker, *The Effective Executive* | Record your time before you manage it. Consolidate it into large chunks. |
| Dan Martell, *Buy Back Your Time* | 2-week Time & Energy Audit, Buyback Rate, DRIP Matrix, Perfect Week, Camcorder Method, 1-3-1 rule |
| Alex Hormozi | Maker vs. manager split, long maker blocks, few meetings a week, deciding what NOT to do, peak hours walled off by the team |
| Paul Graham, *Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule* | One meeting can wreck a whole half-day of maker time |
| Cal Newport, *Deep Work* | Time-block every hour, deep work as a scheduled appointment, shutdown ritual |
| Dan Sullivan, Strategic Coach | Focus, Buffer and Free Days. Unique Ability. *Who Not How* |
| Tim Ferriss, *The 4-Hour Workweek* | Eliminate, then automate, then delegate. Batch similar tasks |
| Jack Dorsey (Twitter/Square) | Theme days: one domain per day |
| Gary Keller, *The ONE Thing* · Brian Tracy, *Eat That Frog* | Do the most important thing first, before anything reactive |
| Keith Cunningham, *The Road Less Stupid* | Scheduled thinking time: one question, one hour, on paper |
| Sophie Leroy (attention residue) · Gloria Mark (UC Irvine) | Switching tasks leaves part of your attention behind; getting back into deep work after an interruption takes about 20+ minutes |
| Daniel Pink, *When* | Peak, trough and rebound hours. Match the type of work to your energy |
| C. Northcote Parkinson | Work expands to fill the time you give it. Book short meetings by default |
| David Allen, *Getting Things Done* | A weekly review closes the loop |

## The Founder Time Flow

The ten laws aren't a list to memorise. They run in order, as one flow. Each stage feeds the next, and the last stage loops back to the first every Friday.

| Stage | Question it answers | Laws | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. SEE** | Where did my time actually go? | 1 | Two weeks of evidence: booked vs. real |
| **2. CUT** | What shouldn't exist at all? | 2 | Dead meetings and stale recurring series deleted |
| **3. PRICE** | What is my hour worth, and which work is below it? | 3, 4 | Buyback Rate, plus every task sorted into DRIP |
| **4. DESIGN** | What should my week look like? | 5, 6, 7, 8 | Ideal week: maker blocks on peak hours, calls clustered by mode, day types |
| **5. TRANSFER** | Who takes what, and how? | 9 | Handoffs with owner, SOP (record it once), 1-3-1 rule |
| **6. DEFEND** | How does it stay fixed? | 10 | Hard stop, thinking time, Friday EA review that loops back to SEE |

The order matters: cut before you price (no point pricing work that shouldn't exist), and price before you design (the design is built from Produce work only).

**Visual for each stage (ideas only; Step 6 is the layout the page must have):**
- **SEE:** the two-week timeline, with real meetings solid and booked-but-never-happened ones as dashed ghosts
- **CUT:** a funnel, Eliminate → Automate → Delegate, with hours dropping out at each step
- **PRICE:** the Buyback Rate shown as an equation card, plus the DRIP 2×2 (money × energy)
- **DESIGN:** a maker block cracked by one meeting (Graham), an energy curve with peak, trough and rebound (Pink), scattered calls sorted into colour lanes (context maxing), and a week strip of Focus / Buffer / Free days (Sullivan)
- **TRANSFER:** Martell's Replacement Ladder (admin → delivery → marketing → sales → leadership), plus a record → SOP → owner pipeline
- **DEFEND:** a circular weekly loop with the Friday review at the top, and a hard-stop line across the day

## The ten laws

### 1. Measure before you manage
Drucker and Martell agree: memory lies about time. Two weeks of evidence beats any opinion.
**Check:** how much of the work week is covered by data (calendar + recordings + email). Below 70%, say so before drawing conclusions.

### 2. Eliminate before you delegate
Ferriss's order (eliminate, then automate, then delegate) and Hormozi's "decide what's not worth doing". Handing off work that shouldn't exist just spreads the waste around.
**Check:** recurring meetings with no recent record of happening, no decision, or no reason left to exist. Calendar events that never happened.

### 3. Price your hour
Martell's Buyback Rate: annual income ÷ 2,000 hours ÷ 4. Anything a person could do for less than that rate gets bought back.
**Check:** hours spent on work below the Buyback Rate (admin, scheduling, bookkeeping, vendor support, first drafts).

### 4. Sort by money and energy
Martell's DRIP Matrix crosses value against energy. Low value + drains you = **Delegate**. High value + drains you = **Replace** (hire someone senior). Low value + energises you = **Invest** (keep, time-boxed). High value + energises you = **Produce** (protect and expand it). Sullivan's Unique Ability is the Produce quadrant.
**Check:** hours in each quadrant. The founder rates categories green or red during onboarding.

### 5. Maker time and manager time never share a block
Graham: a single 30-minute meeting splits a morning into two pieces too small for maker work. Hormozi runs 4–6 hour maker blocks and keeps meetings to a handful a week.
**Check:** maker hours protected vs. planned. The longest uninterrupted block each day. Meetings that landed inside a maker block.

### 6. Peak hours go to the hardest thing, first
Newport, Keller and Tracy: the deep work happens before the reactive day starts. Pink: match the work to your peak. Hormozi's team keeps everyone away from him during his peak hours.
**Check:** share of the founder's peak window that stayed free of meetings, email and Slack.

### 7. Context maxing: group by mental mode
Every switch between kinds of conversation costs attention (Leroy, Mark). So batch calls by the mode they need, not by when the other person was free:
- **Sell** (prospects, partners): energy, persuasion
- **Serve** (clients, delivery): empathy, problem-solving
- **Lead** (team 1:1s, team meetings): coaching, decisions
- **Learn** (coaches, peers, masterminds): input
- **Other ventures**: a different company altogether

Dorsey went further and gave each day one domain. The minimum version: same-mode calls back to back, in one window, on set days.
**Check:** the context-clustering score (share of meetings that sit next to a same-mode meeting), mode switches per day, and how many modes appear on the average day.

### 8. Every day has a type
Sullivan: **Focus Days** (80%+ on the highest-value work), **Buffer Days** (admin, prep, team) and **Free Days** (no work at all, which is what makes the Focus Days good). Martell's Perfect Week and Hyatt's Ideal Week are templates of those day types that the calendar defends on its own.
**Check:** classify each day by what actually happened (Focus / Buffer / Mixed / Free). Count real Free Days. Measure how far the actual week drifted from the founder's ideal week.

### 9. Meetings are short, scheduled and replaceable
Parkinson: a 60-minute default fills 60 minutes. Martell's 1-3-1 rule: bring one problem, three options and one recommendation, so the team brings decisions, not questions. Status updates go async. Anything explained twice gets recorded once and becomes an SOP (Martell's Camcorder Method).
**Check:** meeting-length mix (15/25/50 vs. 30/60). Recurring status meetings that an async update could replace. Topics the founder explained more than once in the recordings.

### 10. Stop, think, review
Newport's shutdown ritual and Sullivan's Free Days protect recovery. Cunningham's weekly Thinking Time is where strategy happens. Allen's weekly review is where the system gets corrected.
**Check:** breaches of the hard stop, at least one thinking block of 1+ hour a week, and a weekly review on the calendar.

## The scorecard

The audit scores each law **On track / Drifting / Broken** against the founder's own targets, set during onboarding. Default targets, used when the founder has none:

| # | Check | Default target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data coverage | ≥ 70% of work hours |
| 2 | Ghost / stale calendar time | ≤ 5% of booked hours |
| 3 | Hours below Buyback Rate | ≤ 5 h / week |
| 4 | Time in Produce quadrant | ≥ 50% of work hours |
| 5 | Maker hours protected | ≥ 12 h / week, longest block ≥ 3 h on 3+ days |
| 6 | Peak window kept clean | ≥ 90% |
| 7 | Context clustering | ≥ 70% of meetings clustered, ≤ 3 modes per day |
| 8 | Free Days | ≥ 1 per week |
| 9 | Meetings at short defaults | ≥ 60% at 15 / 25 / 50 min |
| 10 | Hard-stop breaches | 0 per week; ≥ 1 h of thinking time per week |

A law that is Broken becomes a move in the action plan. A law that is On track gets one line of credit, so the founder can see the system is working too.
